## The short version

Oleg Koval is a lead engineer and fractional CTO with more than ten years of experience shipping
across fintech, e-commerce, mobility, automation, AI, and open source.

His public portfolio describes the operating principles behind that work as explicit contracts,
observable failures, and automation that people can audit. Factory applies those principles to
coding agents: it turns a completion claim into a record another engineer can inspect and an
executable gate can reject.

## Why Factory exists

Coding agents can produce changes quickly. The harder problem is deciding whether those changes
are ready to call delivered without replaying the entire conversation by hand.

Factory is Oleg's answer to failures he no longer wanted to supervise twice. It requires testable
acceptance criteria, isolates changes, proves tests can catch the intended failure, reviews both
milestones and the whole branch, preserves receipts, and ends in one explicit terminal state.

The strongest product claim is deliberately narrow: Factory makes agent delivery claims
falsifiable. It does not claim that a gate can choose the right product requirements, eliminate
human judgment, or prove speed and quality improvements without measured evidence.
